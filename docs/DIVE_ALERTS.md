# Dive Alerts

Dive Settings now has six fixed-height, equally weighted buttons, including **Dive Alerts**. The sidebar has no scroll container. Safety Stop Time appears immediately above Safety Stop Depth. New installations start in Remind only. Previously saved modes, including Off, are preserved.

## Configuration and controls

Dive Alerts opens one compact setup screen, with no nested sidebar or duplicate Page selector. The button column is a compact 384 dp wide, leaving the remaining width for the actual alert preview. All lists fit without scrolling, and hidden/inapplicable settings are excluded from hardware focus order. Back buttons show only BACK. Changes save automatically. Back returns one level and highlights the next Back button, including Back to camera in Dive Settings; up from the first row wraps directly to Back.

- **Alert type:** Off, Remind only, Track air, or Live receiver. Remind only is the initial mode; one right press enables manual pressure tracking. Off shows only Alert type and Back, with no preview, timing, appearance or test controls, and suppresses all air reminders and gas warnings. Remind only has four rows in release builds: alert type, timing, appearance, and Back. Track air adds confirmation cadence and Tank & display. Live receiver automatically searches and lists nearby devices, with fixed Alert type, Scan again and Back controls. Tap a device, or highlight it with Up/Down and press OK, to connect. Remaining setup controls appear after a successful receiver connection.
- **When:** adjust by 30 seconds with left/right or one minute with Z± directly on the main screen. OK opens Repeat / Once / Sequence. The detailed interval accepts direct numeric seconds (30 seconds to 6 hours). A sequence accepts up to 24 minute marks (1–360), each with its own message and choice of default behaviour, reminder only, or pressure input. Select an entry with left/right, change its time with Z±, or enter it using OK and the keypad. Moving an entry preserves its message and input choice. Add opens the keypad; cancelling does not add an entry. Duplicate times are rejected. No inactive interval/list controls remain on screen.
- **Ask for pressure:** left/right sets every alert, every second alert, and so on (up to 100). OK opens the specific-alert list (up to 24 alert numbers, 1–999). Receiver mode also permits Never / display only. The list uses the same entry controls as specific times. A one-time manual tracking alert asks once and hides the irrelevant cadence row; a one-time receiver alert offers a simple yes/no confirmation choice. Simple reminders are selected using Alert type, without a redundant confirmation-off setting.
- **Message & style:** save a default message of up to 64 characters. Sequence entries can override it independently. Text can be typed on the phone before the dive, or entered with the housing: left/right selects a character, Z± jumps five characters, OK adds it, down deletes, Shutter saves, and up cancels. Visible for combines automatic duration and manual dismissal. Left/right sets 5–120 seconds; OK toggles automatic hide / until dismissed. Colour, opacity (20–100%), and flashing are separate controls. **Your Alert** renders the same component as the real alert, scaled to fit; the sequence editor previews the selected entry, including its text and pressure slider.
- **Tank & display:** BAR/PSIG, confirmed starting pressure, slider minimum/maximum, surface reserve, and camera readout. Slider limits save in canonical BAR and display in the chosen unit. They apply to the reminder slider, its housing adjustments, and the suggested slider position; setup entries retain the full range. Limits must leave at least 1 BAR between them. Direct keypad readings can still report an actual gauge value outside the slider range. Saving the actual starting gauge reading confirms it for the next dive in the same step; there is no separate arm button. Starting confirmation is consumed once and is never persisted across restarts. Tank/source changes are locked during a dive; alert timing, appearance, and disabling alerts remain available.
- **Live receiver device list:** entering the disconnected receiver view starts one bounded search automatically. Devices appear as individual rows with names and addresses; tapping a row connects to that exact address immediately. Housing Up/Down traverses each device, keeps the focused row visible and OK connects. Only the device list can scroll; Alert type, Scan again and Back remain fixed, and the Dive Settings sidebar remains non-scrollable. New discoveries preserve device/Back focus. A saved mapping is retained for the same receiver, but the picker never connects merely because a device is saved. Scan again explicitly retries a completed/failed search; connection failures remain visible without repeated automatic searches. A successful GATT connection and service discovery reveal data/mapping controls; progress, no-device, timeout, discovery and connection failures are shown explicitly. The selected signal starts reading automatically. Disconnecting hides the advanced receiver controls again. Connected setup offers pressure-byte mapping and two-point gauge verification, plus separately labelled values.

**Remind only** is centred vertically and horizontally. It displays **CHECK YOUR AIR** (or the saved custom message), DIVE TIME and NEXT ALERT as elapsed dive-clock values, and one **OK** button. The next time comes from the same schedule used by delivery and the debug simulator; a completed once/sequence schedule shows None. OK or the housing dismissal controls close it. Colour, flashing, opacity and duration remain configurable. The preview uses the identical component at a representative scheduled dive time. The central housing-connection banner temporarily yields while an air alert occupies the centre; the top connection indicator remains visible.

The active depth profile starts the alert timer. Missed deadlines coalesce to one current alert after a menu or safety stop is cleared. Skipping, auto-hiding and testing never count as pressure readings.

During a confirmation alert, the pressure slider starts at the expected pressure (or the saved starting pressure until consumption is known). The banner places LAST ALERT and EXPECTED side by side, in a compact centered group with small BAR/PSIG units aligned to the numbers' baseline and ESTIMATED BOTTOM TIME below. If the prediction falls outside the configured slider range, the bounded selection is shown explicitly as blue TANK PRESSURE and drives the live bottom-time preview; EXPECTED remains the uncapped prediction. The pressure slider stays blue and uses a rounded, ribbed housing-style handle labelled Z+/−. Moving it changes the left label to TANK PRESSURE and shows both the unsaved value and label in the same blue; the expected pressure remains independent. Bottom time recalculates immediately from the draft pressure, including the first valid pressure drop, without saving an observation until OK. Starting-pressure setup is labelled Default Start Pressure until edited, then Pressure Now. Unavailable estimates show a dash. **Left/right** changes it by 1 BAR or 10 PSIG; **Z+/Z−** changes it by 5 BAR or 5 PSIG. **OK** reports the displayed pressure immediately, so an accurate estimate needs one click and a one-step correction needs two. **Up** opens the optional keypad and **down** skips. A reminder-only alert leaves the zoom slider available for the camera. Shutter remains the camera shutter until pressure editing starts, then it also saves.

The optional keypad has a 3×3 grid for 1–9 and a 0/delete/save footer. Arrows select; OK enters a digit; the first digit replaces the suggestion. Back returns to the slider without losing the value. Shutter saves from any cell. Adjusting the slider or opening the keypad suspends automatic dismissal. Save rejects invalid entries without closing the editor. Reporting pressure saves the reading and closes the alert immediately. There is no follow-up receipt or consumption-comparison popup.

### Alert validation

There is no notification test button in debug or release settings. The ViewModel does not enable the internal simulation controls. **Your Alert** remains an accurate settings preview; it does not start a dive or report pressure.

Live alerts run independently of that preview. The ViewModel calls `ControlCore.tickDiveProfile()` every 500 ms. Actual housing depth packets start/update the dive; the alert engine uses that session and the saved schedule. `emitOutcome()` publishes the resulting alert state to the shared overlay, routes receiver requests and critical audio, and persists setting changes. Both touch actions and decoded housing buttons feed the same command reducer. A fresh surface reading ends the session and clears its pressure prompt/history.

`AirAlertEndToEndTest` exercises settings serialization, raw sensor packets, production timer ticks, housing packet decoding, recurring/once/sequence schedules, confirmation cadence, pressure learning/readout, missing receiver data, blocked presentation and surfacing. `AirAlertWiringInstrumentation` uses an isolated preferences file and in-memory core on the phone to verify the actual Compose alert and touch callbacks. Neither depends on the retired notification test action. Synthetic sensor/receiver inputs do not validate physical BLE compatibility or underwater operation. The older simulator remains an internal automated-test fixture only.

Pressure entry uses reversed Z controls: Z+ lowers pressure and Z− raises pressure, including the first adjustment from a reminder and pressure-keypad entry. This also applies to starting-pressure and slider-range editors. Left/right retains fine decrease/increase control; Z controls outside pressure entry retain their existing direction.

## Camera readout

Camera readout offers six choices, in this order: estimated bottom time remaining; last manually confirmed tank pressure with its age; pulse between bottom time and last pressure; live receiver pressure; pulse between bottom time and live pressure; and None / Off. Left/right, Z± or OK cycles the options directly, with no information popup. Pulse alternates every four seconds of real time with a short crossfade. Manual history remains separate when receiver readings arrive. Live pressure requires a verified, connected, fresh receiver signal; missing or stale data is explicitly unavailable. The readout floats independently between the heading/depth pill and the centre camera-mode button. Showing, hiding or alternating it never changes the position of the depth, heading, temperature, navigation arrow or camera controls. Bottom time is hidden until an active dive has sufficient consumption and depth data, including during the bottom-time phase of a pulse mode. A valid zero-minute estimate is still displayed. Pressure modes can appear before a dive; last pressure shows NO READING until a pressure has been confirmed. The display clock runs independently of the dive timer, including at the surface. Off hides only this readout.

Version 5 settings preserve prior preferences and drop the retired manual ascent value. Earlier visible readouts migrate to time/last-pressure pulse; hidden readouts remain Off. Version 4 readout selections also migrate unchanged.

## Estimates

All internal pressures are gauge pressure in BAR. PSIG is converted using 14.503773773 psi/bar. A single-cylinder pressure budget does not require tank volume; this is not a volume-based SAC/RMV reading.

The start-pressure setting is only a suggestion until the diver explicitly confirms it for the next dive. That confirmation is used once and is not persisted across app restarts. With that baseline, the first consumption reading can produce an estimate. Otherwise two timed pressure measurements are needed. Unchanged pressure cannot create an infinite time estimate.

Consumption is normalized using integrated ambient pressure from fresh depth samples: pressure drop divided by the integral of `(1 + depth / 10) × minutes`. A rate requires at least 30 seconds and more than 0.5 BAR pressure drop. The next predicted pressure subtracts consumption over the measured depth history. At the same tank pressure and learned consumption rate, a deeper current depth reduces the remaining time and increases the ascent allowance. A correction updates the rate and identifies consumption above the previous estimate. A pressure rise greater than 2 BAR resets the rate as a possible refill/tank change. Switching between manual and receiver observations starts a new consumption interval.

Ascent is automatic; there is no ascent setting or OK information panel. The calculation uses the existing rolling vertical-speed measurement from depth samples after at least three seconds of continuous data. It accepts upward movement of at least 0.6 m/min, excludes holding-depth noise and descent, and requires a sample newer than three seconds. The gas-budget fallback is 6 m/min. A slower measured ascent increases the ascent air allowance; the planning rate is capped at 6 m/min so faster movement cannot inflate bottom time. Both ascent legs use this rate. This is a conservative air-budget design assumption, not a prescribed ascent speed or a claim of medical safety: [DAN explains that no single safest ascent rate is established](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/ascent-rates/). Sensor gaps continue to withhold bottom-time estimates until the existing depth/consumption checks recover.

Gas time holds back the configured surface reserve plus estimated gas for ascent to the configured stop depth, the configured safety stop, and ascent to the surface. It reports minutes remaining at the current depth **before ascent**, not minutes until running out of gas. The full configured stop is reserved even if part has been completed. It is a gas-only advisory, not an NDL, decompression plan, shared-gas emergency reserve calculation, or authorization to remain at depth. The diver must still follow their dive computer and gas plan.

A depth gap or processing gap invalidates the consumption interval; new readings are needed to establish a new rate. The gas-time estimate is withheld during known decompression obligations or after the profile reaches its no-decompression limit. Receiver pressure is never labelled LIVE after its freshness timeout or disconnect. Manual readings remain available as a fallback. Every new dive starts with a fresh observation history. App restarts retain configuration, but deliberately discard live data, estimates, and prior gauge confirmations.

## Gas warnings and second tanks

While pressure tracking is active, automatic gas warnings monitor the estimated pressure at the surface independently of the reminder schedule. The exit budget includes ascent and the remaining configured safety stop; only measured completed stop time is credited. At the configured reserve, the warning directs the diver toward their planned safety stop (or planned ascent when no stop applies). **Check pressure**, **Switch tank**, **Buddy air**, and **Skip** share one housing-selectable action row. Skipping schedules another warning in 30 seconds of dive time, shortening to 15 seconds below half the reserve, then escalating to the repeating critical alarm at the protected margin. A new, more urgent level appears immediately. Confirmed readings update the risk; unsaved slider values do not.

A projected surface pressure approaching a protected margin produces **OUT OF AIR RISK**; only a confirmed zero gauge reading produces **TANK REPORTED EMPTY**. Critical warnings override ordinary alert presentation, repeat the audible alarm and offline speech, and cannot be skipped. They stay active through lost data and editing until fresh depth measures the surface (0.05 m or less), or a tank switch or established buddy-air supply is explicitly confirmed. Selecting Alert type Off explicitly clears all air warnings, including a latched critical alarm. Gas-only warnings cannot calculate a new exit budget when consumption/depth data is insufficient or a decompression obligation is known; an already-critical alarm stays latched. These are supplementary estimates, not a guarantee of breathable gas or safe ascent.

The critical speech says **“Out of air risk”** with a gauge/buddy check; an actual reported zero says **“Out of air”**. It does not direct a diver to perform a safety stop with insufficient gas: [DAN advises against a stop that would exhaust the breathing supply](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/avoiding-panic-after-regulator-failure/). Known decompression obligations remain explicitly identified. Alarm volume is temporarily raised and restored; a user's volume change is preserved. Offline speech depends on an installed English voice, and the repeating alarm remains available if speech fails. Phone audio has not been validated underwater or through a housing.

**Switch tank** opens a single pressure slider, prefilled from the default starting pressure. **OK — SWITCH CONFIRMED** declares that the diver has switched, verified gas delivery, and entered the new gauge reading; cancel leaves the original tank and warning intact. This supports another tank with the same breathing gas as the current plan, not a decompression-gas switch. The entered pressure must exceed the configured reserve. The new tank gets a separate baseline and consumption history; the last eight prior tank records remain in runtime state. Bottom time is hidden until new-tank consumption is measured. Follow-up pressure checks start after 30 seconds and repeat until a rate can be learned, even for a once-only schedule. The old receiver is ignored for the new tank and any receiver readout explicitly becomes manual; no transmitter reassignment is assumed. Opening the screen alone never silences a critical warning. Test tank switches stay inside the isolated debug dive and do not change saved settings.

### Earlier critical warning and confirmed buddy air

The critical trigger now preserves time to react: `projected pressure at surface <= max(10 PSIG in BAR, 25% of configured surface reserve) + one minute of estimated consumption at current depth`. The projected pressure already deducts ascent and the remaining stop at 6 m/min, or a fresh slower measured ascent. Faster measured ascent cannot postpone the warning. A confirmed reading at or below 10 PSIG also triggers critical risk even without a learned rate or a working depth sensor during an active dive; zero remains “tank reported empty.” The 60-second allowance and quarter-reserve margin are explicit engineering assumptions, not validated emergency gas planning. **10 PSIG is never presented as a safe surface reserve or guaranteed outcome.** The earlier reserve alert still uses the full configured reserve. [DAN's gas-planning guidance](https://world.dan.org/health-medicine/health-resource/smart-guides/13-ways-to-run-out-of-air-how-not-to/safety-tips-to-prevent-out-of-air-emergencies/) emphasizes retaining emergency gas and warns that a buddy may also be low.

A critical warning minimizes the **optional safety-stop panel** and says that an optional stop depends on available gas. It does not change the configured stop duration, mark time complete, or clear a decompression obligation. A required ceiling or latched NDL obligation retains its presentation. Confirmed alternate gas restores the existing stop presentation; gas sharing by itself never proves enough gas for a stop. Follow the dive computer and training, with [DAN's distinction between a precautionary stop and insufficient breathing gas](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/avoiding-panic-after-regulator-failure/).

**Buddy air** opens one confirmation: confirm only after breathing from the buddy's alternate air source, using the breathing gas in the current plan. Opening/cancelling it leaves the alarm active. Confirmation silences the original-tank alarm and shows **BUDDY AIR · GAS UNKNOWN**. Original-tank receiver readings, pressure prompts and bottom-time estimates are suspended: the app has no donor-cylinder volume, pressure or two-diver consumption data. Check the donor's gauge and end the dive according to training. **OK — OWN TANK** opens the existing pressure/switch-confirmation flow to resume a verified independent supply. Surface detection and a new dive clear shared-air state. Debug shared-air changes remain isolated from the actual dive and settings.

## Experimental receiver setup

This is a separate Android GATT connection with its own operation queue. It does not disconnect or reconfigure the camera housing. Scanning is bounded to 12 seconds; connect/service discovery is bounded to 20 seconds. Selecting a discovered device connects directly and starts reading its first or previously mapped signal after service discovery. Choose a different readable/notifying characteristic if needed. Readable characteristics are polled every two seconds. Only notification configuration (CCCD) is written; no arbitrary device commands or firmware writes are sent. The known housing address and DIVEIT advertising name are excluded.

Raw candidate bytes and a decoded candidate are displayed during setup. Supported formats are unsigned 8/16/24/32-bit integers and IEEE float32, either byte order, with a selectable byte offset, multiplier, additive offset, and BAR/PSIG source units. The formula is `(raw × multiplier + offset) / unitsPerBar`. Packets that are too short, invalid, non-finite, or outside 0–350 BAR are rejected. This supports accessible, documented or experimentally identified scalar GATT values; it does not decrypt proprietary streams, infer arbitrary vendor protocols, or read 38 kHz radio.

**Label other values** stores up to eight named signals with an optional unit label, characteristic, numeric format, byte order, byte offset, multiplier and additive offset. Live decoded values and raw bytes appear during setup. Signed/negative results can be expressed via float32 or an additive offset; arbitrary protocol decoding is not inferred. Their mappings and labels persist, but samples do not. They expire with the receiver freshness setting, are scoped to the selected device, and never feed pressure or dive calculations. Saved signals are read/subscribed alongside pressure using the receiver’s separate GATT queue.

Two fresh gauge comparisons, at least 5 BAR apart and within the larger of 2 BAR or 2%, are required before candidate bytes can be displayed as live pressure. This is a mapping sanity check, not equipment certification or proof of underwater communication reliability. Changing the device, characteristic, format, scale, offset, byte order, or unit clears verification. Obtain different pressure samples through normal equipment use; never alter pressurized equipment for this test. Verified mappings persist and can reconnect at app startup, with up to three retries after a dropped connection. The RX / RECEIVER label means recently received Bluetooth bytes; a generic mapping cannot determine whether a receiver is replaying an older tank reading. A vendor timestamp/status protocol is needed to establish end-to-end transmitter freshness. A failed connection remains explicitly unavailable and the manual flow remains usable.

### Swift / Tern TX findings

- [Shearwater Swift](https://shearwater.com/en-ca/products/swift-transmitter) sends tank pressure to compatible dive computers using 38 kHz radio. A phone Bluetooth radio and the documented Weefine WFH07 BLE interface cannot receive that signal directly. The [project housing protocol](BLE_PROTOCOL.md) has environmental/vacuum pressure and button channels, not an air-integration radio channel.
- The [Tern TX manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0838/3732/1530/files/TernTX_Manual_Rev_B.pdf?v=1709841056), pages 42 and 60, documents its air-integration receiver and Bluetooth firmware/log transfers. It does not establish a live pressure GATT characteristic or live forwarding during a dive. The [current support page](https://shearwater.com/pages/tern-tx-support) and linked v36 release notes did not establish that capability either.
- A surface investigation can reveal the watch's readable/notifying characteristics. Finding a BLE device, a battery value, or historical log bytes is not evidence of live tank pressure. A compatible 38 kHz-to-BLE hardware bridge or a manufacturer-supported live interface would be needed for the requested live integration.

## Verification

`AirAlertsTest` and `AirAlertFlowTest` cover scheduling, confirmation cadence, skipped/expired prompts, navigation, pressure entry, unit conversion, consumption and reserve math, depth gaps, new dives, receiver freshness, byte decoding, calibration, and settings persistence. `AirSimulationTest` covers the accelerated clock, configured schedules/cadence, pressure learning, shared slider/skip controls, test isolation and release/dive guards. `AirReadingEstimateTest` covers live draft estimates, unchanged history/expected pressure until confirmation, cancellation, units, receiver precedence and withheld estimates. `AirSliderRangeTest` covers range configuration, unit conversion, legacy settings migration, bounds validation, touch/housing clamping, suggested pressure and direct gauge entry. `AirAlertSequenceTest` covers messages, per-entry behaviour, moving/removing entries, housing text entry and migration of existing settings. Build and unit-test results are recorded in the task response. Physical Swift/Tern TX streaming and underwater radio reliability require hardware verification and are not represented as working features.

`AirGasWarningTest` covers reserve boundaries, accelerating recurrence, critical latching and surface release, stop-time credit, tank-switch confirmation/cancellation, receiver isolation, housing navigation, and finite debug schedules.
