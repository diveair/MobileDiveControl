# Dive profile, safety stop and decompression tracking

## Live diving and the finite motion exercise

Normal launches use genuine pressure readings with ascent triggering enabled and a default 5 m target. The old 0 m surface checkpoint is archived on migration; it cannot keep the timer looping at the surface.

The debug-only launch extra `divecontrol.safety_stop_motion=true` explicitly runs one finite 43.5-second exercise through the actual pressure tracker and UI. It descends to 30 m, ascends to the configured target, holds, rises 1.2 m above the target, then descends back to target and finishes at the surface. It demonstrates red ascent, yellow recovery and green arrival, without an alternate banner or forced colors. It refuses to run during an active real dive. Simulated physiology and logs are not persisted. The previous monitoring state is restored when the exercise ends; real water readings resume automatically. Other housing telemetry and controls continue during the exercise.

## Navigation and presentation

From Track Heading in the camera mode menu, Up selects Dive Settings and another Up selects Diagnostics. While a stop is actually running, Safety Stop appears immediately below Track Heading and restores the expanded banner. The entry disappears after completion. Existing capture-mode indices are stable.

Dive Settings puts the profile/log on the left and its fixed settings menu on the right. Dive Logs is the first setting; housing Left/Right, wheel and touch browse up to 1,000 saved sessions. Gas, O2/He fractions, target and duration are captured and locked during an active profile. There is no manual stop-requirement setting: actual depth and ascent determine triggering, and recorded depth/NDL determine the known profile flags. All five main-menu options share the available screen height and stay visible without scrolling. Labels and values are centered on each card, including the gas row with its balanced submenu chevron. The graph uses a labeled depth axis increasing downward, elapsed-time ticks, depth scaling matched to the dive, and separate shaded segments across monitoring gaps. The summary row shows MAX, NDL, TIME and stop status. The former yellow text block beneath it is removed. Current NDL appears on live profiles; saved logs show the minimum valid NDL recorded during that dive. Legacy logs without recorded NDL show a dash. Initialized calculations also appear beneath the camera depth readout.

The single expanded banner contains a same-size heading such as `5.0m SAFETY STOP`, centered time, and centered current depth to one decimal place. There is no @ label, separate TARGET DEPTH line, TIMING BAND line, HOLDING DEPTH line, TOO DEEP line or OK instruction. The timer's timing band and the color tolerance are separate: within target ±0.25 m the box is green, shallower is red with a slow-descent instruction, and deeper is yellow.

Motion is estimated over five seconds. Full-width filled triangles point up above the box during ascent and down below it during descent. Direction and rate overlay the triangle as `WARNING ASCENDING` / `WARNING DESCENDING` and one-decimal m/sec (ft/sec with imperial units). Beyond 0.25 m shallower than target, an ascending or stationary diver sees red, including the underlying banner edges. The ascent warning flashes yellow and black every 500 ms. Detected descent toward the target changes the UI to yellow, then the entire card and triangle turn green within target +/-0.25 m. Exact tolerance boundaries stay green. Deeper than the target tolerance is yellow. The timing band is separate from this tighter visual tolerance.

OK minimizes an active timer at the top center. OK dismisses completion. The same card can be tapped, and the active-only center-menu entry expands the timer. Completion automatically expands a minimized stop and displays a green check and successful timer-completion message. These actions preserve earned time and work without starting the pump or changing camera recording state.

Start and completion use maximum alarm-stream volume, synthesized tones and amplitude-255 vibration waveforms. Start tone duration is 1.8 seconds; completion is 2.4 seconds. Offline English speech says exactly `BEGIN SAFETY STOP` and `SAFETY STOP FINISHED`, at reduced speaking rate. The previous volume is restored after playback if the user has not changed it. Device logs confirmed both spoken phrases finished. Acoustic effectiveness through the housing underwater has not been measured.

The settings column is 126 dp wide, 40% narrower than the previous 210 dp column. Left/right chevrons replace the minus/plus controls. On Dive Logs, LEFT/RIGHT browse and OK opens the selected saved log's menu. Delete Dive Log lives inside that menu; selecting it opens a separate confirmation identifying the saved dive, with Cancel selected by default. LEFT/RIGHT choose Cancel/Delete and OK confirms. Cancelling returns to the log menu. The active dive cannot be deleted. Saved-log deletion never clears physiological history. Breathing gas opens a submenu with OK; Air is fixed at 21% oxygen and 0% helium, Nitrox exposes oxygen, and Trimix exposes oxygen and helium. The main menu has no Dive history row. The one-time confirmation is available again by tapping NDL when starting history is unknown.

Profile points use their recorded elapsed times, including unequal sampling intervals. The chart starts at the first actual reading, retains monitoring gaps, and ends at the final recorded surface depth. Archiving always records that last surface point, even between the regular five-second logging intervals.

## Normal safety-stop rules

- Start the profile at 1.5 m. A fresh reading that rounds to 0.0 m ends and archives the dive (up to 0.05 m sensor tolerance).
- Recommend a safety stop after every dive that went deeper than the configured stop target, including shallow recreational dives. Known deep or computed-limit flags come from recorded maximum depth and valid NDL, and do not suppress the recommendation on other dives. Retired manual flags in legacy saves are consumed and ignored.
- Default to 5 m for 180 seconds, configurable to targets 3-6 m and durations 180/300 seconds.
- Arm after going deeper than the target. Start only on an observed ascent reaching or crossing that target; descent through the target never starts the timer. A direct return to 0.0 m archives an incomplete stop instead of starting a surface timer.
- Count within target +/-1 m, clipped to 3-6 m. Timing tolerance and the tighter +/-0.25 m visual tolerance are implementation choices.
- Credit only consecutive ordered fresh pressure readings, both in the band, less than three seconds apart. No wall-clock tick grants stop time.
- Pause outside the band, on stale/malformed/invalid readings and on disconnect. Preserve earned time without credit across gaps. An unfinished deeper excursion does not restart the timer.
- Completion stays completed without repeated alerts. Rearm only on fresh renewed descent deeper than both target +0.25 m and the shallowest depth since completion +0.25 m. The next observed ascent to target starts a new full stop. This tolerance prevents minor drift from rearming a completed stop.
- Surfacing clears the active stop; the next dive has its own profile and maximum. Restoring a completed checkpoint preserves completion and dismissal. Restoring an unfinished checkpoint resets time credit because the intervening depth was not monitored.

The safety-stop countdown is distinct from a mandatory decompression ceiling. A valid positive ceiling pauses safety-stop credit, forces the existing banner to show a red decompression/ceiling message, and prevents a green completion or OK dismissal from hiding that obligation. Credit resumes only after a fresh interval bracketed by cleared readings; the clearance sample does not credit the previous paused interval. If monitoring is interrupted after a known ceiling, the hold remains with an unavailable-ceiling message. Restarting reconstructs this hold from the retained tissue checkpoint. This is not a staged decompression schedule or a time-to-surface planner. Completion never erases tissue loading or oxygen exposure.

## NDL, ceilings and oxygen

`Buhlmann.kt` implements open-circuit ZH-L16C, all 16 nitrogen and helium compartments, pressure-weighted mixed-gas a/b coefficients, and exact Schreiner integration for linear pressure changes. `expm1` stabilizes short sensor intervals. Default gradient factors are 40/85. NDL projects a 9 m/min ascent on the selected gas and checks both the ascent path and surface arrival against GF high. It reports additional whole seconds at the current depth, capped at 99 minutes. The displayed minute value rounds down; the displayed ceiling rounds up to a tenth of the selected depth unit.

The decompression ceiling uses a retained first-stop anchor rounded up to a 3 m multiple of the GF-low ceiling, with GF increasing linearly to GF high at the surface. This is a ceiling calculation, not a complete staged decompression schedule or time-to-surface planner. There are no CCR/setpoint, altitude, salinity or in-dive gas-switch controls. Configuration currently follows the established depth conversion: surface 1.01325 bar, freshwater 0.0981 bar/m, and water-vapour pressure 0.0627 bar. The settings display identifies the model, gradient factors and sea-level/freshwater assumption.

Oxygen exposure includes current ppO2 in ATA, NOAA CNS percentage, and rolling 24-hour OTU. CNS rate is interpolated between NOAA table knots and integrated exactly across pressure ramps; pulmonary OTU uses the 5/6-power formula. Above 1.6 ATA the CNS table is not extrapolated: the accumulated table-rate value is explicitly displayed as a lower bound, retaining an out-of-range exposure flag. CNS recovery uses a 90-minute half-time during confirmed surface air intervals. A selected oxygen-rich gas is not silently changed to air at shallow depth. OTU uses minute bins and retains the entire boundary minute, conservatively over-retaining by less than a minute.

Tissue and oxygen state persist independently of the 1,000-log retention limit. First use and legacy checkpoints have unknown history and show dashes for NDL/ceiling/CNS/OTU. A one-time pre-dive confirmation lets the diver explicitly declare no diving or supplemental oxygen exposure in the preceding 48 hours, with a fresh surface reading, before initializing equilibrium tissues. An unmonitored interval after at least 60 seconds at the surface requires confirmation that the entire interval was spent at the surface breathing air before off-gassing is credited. An underwater monitoring gap, clock inconsistency or unlogged immersion suppresses numerical limits; it never silently resets tissues to a clean dive. Current ppO2 can still be computed from a fresh pressure reading and the declared gas.

Surface intervals use the actual elapsed time. Continuous readings integrate every nitrogen and helium compartment at the observed pressure on the declared gas. Across a restart or housing disconnection, the saved integration timestamp identifies the uncredited interval; after the surface-air confirmation, the model integrates that entire interval on air and advances the timestamp exactly once. The next dive starts from those residual compartment pressures, not from equilibrium or the previous dive's unchanged final loading. CNS surface recovery and rolling OTU expiry also use elapsed time. Regression tests replay the independent repetitive-air fixture both continuously and across a restart, compare 30/60/120-minute intervals and subsequent NDL, and verify that repeated confirmations/restarts cannot double-credit nitrogen, helium or CNS recovery.


## Persistence and housing shutdown

AtomicFile stores a bounded version-4 checkpoint in the existing `dive-profile-v1.bin` location. Versions 1-3 remain readable; older logs have no recorded minimum NDL. Version-1 settings and logs migrate with uninitialized physiology; no tissue loading is fabricated. The byte limit is 64 MB, sufficient for 1,000 full 3,600-point logs, one active profile, and tissue/oxygen history. At capacity, archiving replaces only the oldest saved log. A full-capacity codec test verifies every log's point count, endpoints and NDL after reloading. Writes are conflated at five-second intervals plus teardown, so abrupt process death can lose the latest five seconds. Old monotonic timestamps are never restored. Each profile keeps up to 3,600 plotted samples, reducing long profiles while preserving gaps. Monitoring runs while the app's ViewModel lives.

After a verified post-dive surface vent, a centered banner offers SHUT DOWN on the left and RESTART PUMP on the right. SHUT DOWN is selected by default, including after process restart and each new vent. Left/Right select a choice; touching either tile only selects it. With SHUT DOWN selected, short and repeated OK events are consumed without starting the pump; the housing firmware handles holding OK to power off. With RESTART PUMP selected, a short OK explicitly starts the normal seal-check workflow with cover-open gating, motor timeout and automatic stop conditions. Restart is blocked when disconnected, when the cover is closed/unknown or when the available water-pressure reading indicates submersion. Direct pump-start commands and seal-reset bypasses remain blocked while the choice is pending. Reconnection resets the selection and awaits fresh cover telemetry.

After reconnecting with the vacuum released and the cap open, the normal pump-start banner reads `INSERT PHONE INTO HOUSING THEN`, `PRESS MENU/OK TO START VACUUM PUMP`, and `DOWN dismisses`. OK enters the managed seal-check workflow; DOWN dismisses the prompt. A still-established vacuum is adopted instead of offering a pump start.

## Sources and verification

Reference sources checked 2026-09-23/24:

- [DecoTengu model documentation](https://wrobell.dcmod.org/decotengu/model.html): Bühlmann/Schreiner equations and gradient factors, with links to Erik Baker's original papers.
- [NOAA oxygen-limit research and table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12500339/).
- [Shearwater Perdix manual](https://www.shearwater.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Perdix-UserManual-SI-DocRevD.pdf): NOAA oxygen clock and 90-minute surface CNS recovery.
- [DAN defensive profile planning](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/defensive-dive-profile-planning/): safety-stop practice and the benefit of conservative multilevel profiles.
- [DAN rescue guidance](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/rescue-of-an-unconscious-diver/): distinction between an optional safety stop and a decompression obligation.

`tools/deco_reference_fixtures.py` generates independent fixtures with DecoTengu 0.14.1 as a test-only reference dependency. Reference library code is not bundled in the app. Ten profiles cover Air, Nitrox, Trimix, staged gas switches in the numerical engine, repetitive dives and very short pressure ramps. All 32 tissue pressures and GF40/GF85 ceilings agree within 1e-9 bar; whole-second NDL matches the reference cases. Additional tests cover oxygen dose integration, monitoring gaps, helium, offline intervals, clock changes, persistence and legacy migration.

Safety-stop tests cover threshold boundaries, descent rejection, ascent triggering, valid time credit, drift, missing/out-of-order packets, restart, actual surface trigger, minimization, menu restoration and completion dismissal. Housing-choice tests cover default shutdown, repeated OK, left/right selection, explicit restart, cover and link guards, submersion, reconnect and new-vent reset. Phone verification covered the full and minimized UI, active-only menu restoration, speech completion, same-size heading and filled warning triangles. Real vent/power-cycle behavior and underwater audio remain unverified on hardware.
