# Dive profile, safety stop and decompression tracking

## Live diving and the finite motion exercise

### Windows USB depth controller

Open **Depth Lab.cmd** on the laptop and select **Connect via USB**. The controller uses the USB-connected Android device and opens **DiveControl Depth Lab** on it. Enable USB debugging, accept the laptop’s debugging authorization, and leave Wireless debugging off. Power on the housing with Bluetooth enabled on the handset. The lab app connects to the real housing normally. The desktop shows its Bluetooth state, fresh housing-sensor depth, selected depth source and the live depth used by the app.

Use **Set** for an exact depth or choose **Ascend**, **Hold** or **Descend** with a rate in metres per minute. **Start a 5 m stop** replays a surface sample, a qualifying descent to 12 m and an ascent back to the default target. It operates on the current lab dive. Use the housing’s physical OK/MENU or the laptop button to minimize. Use 6.5 m for the deep pause and 7.0 m for the reserve confirmation. Return to 5.5 m to re-arm the episode. Use 3.5 m for the shallow warning and return to 5.0 m to resume timing. **Simulate depth loss** suspends depth updates while both connections stay available. **Use real housing depth** hands the display back to the next fresh Bluetooth pressure sample. Disconnecting USB, closing the controller or unplugging the cable also releases the override.

The laptop drives only the test app’s input stream. The housing’s raw BLE pressure samples continue to reach the radio monitor, raw diagnostics and pump interlock. Hardware buttons and other characteristics follow their usual route. Pressure uses the housing’s 0.01 kPa packet resolution. The app’s measured direction uses the same five-second history as a physical dive.

Build the lab with `gradlew.bat :app:assembleDepthLab`. The phone installs it alongside DiveControl under `com.mobiledivecontrol.depthlab`, with separate app data. Standard debug and release APKs exclude the lab USB protocol. To disable the entire lab variant at build time, pass `-PincludeDepthLab=false`. To remove its source, remove `app/src/depthLab`, `app/src/testDepthLab`, `tools/depth_lab` and the Windows launcher. The regular build and production export work without those directories. Uninstalling **DiveControl Depth Lab** removes the test application and its lab data from the handset.

Normal launches use genuine pressure readings with ascent triggering enabled and a default 5 m target. The old 0 m surface checkpoint is archived on migration; it cannot keep the timer looping at the surface.

The debug-only launch extra `divecontrol.safety_stop_motion=true` runs a 51.5-second exercise through the pressure tracker and UI. It descends to 30 m, ascends to the configured target, holds, rises 1.6 m above the target, then descends back to target and finishes at the surface. The exercise runs only when there is no active dive. Simulated physiology and logs are discarded. Other housing telemetry and controls remain available throughout the exercise. Real pressure monitoring resumes with the previous state when the exercise ends.

## Navigation and presentation

From Track Heading in the camera mode menu, Up selects Dive Settings and another Up selects Diagnostics. While a stop is actually running, Safety Stop appears immediately below Track Heading and restores the expanded banner. The entry disappears after completion. Existing capture-mode indices are stable.

Dive Settings puts the profile/log on the left and its fixed settings menu on the right. Dive Logs is the first setting; housing Left/Right, wheel and touch browse up to 1,000 saved sessions. Gas, O2/He fractions, target and duration are captured and locked during an active profile. There is no manual stop-requirement setting: actual depth and ascent determine triggering, and recorded depth/NDL determine the known profile flags. All main-menu options share the available screen height and stay visible without scrolling. Labels and values are centered on each card, including the gas row with its balanced submenu chevron. The graph uses a labeled depth axis increasing downward, elapsed-time ticks, depth scaling matched to the dive, and separate shaded segments across monitoring gaps. The summary row shows MAX, NDL, TIME and stop status. The former yellow text block beneath it is removed. Current NDL appears on live profiles; saved logs show the minimum valid NDL recorded during that dive. Legacy logs without recorded NDL show a dash. The camera HUD keeps current depth continuously visible with its small now label. The second compact slot alternates max depth/max and live remaining time/NDL: each holds for three seconds, fades out over 800 ms, swaps its value and label together, then fades in over 800 ms. Both presentations share a measured width so adjacent readings stay still. The time shows seconds below a minute, whole minutes rounded down otherwise, and ∞min at the calculation cap (the calculation itself remains capped at 99 minutes). The pulse runs only while valid NDL is available, including zero seconds, and measured depth is greater than 1 metre. At 1 metre or shallower, max depth stays steady; descending below 1 metre restarts the cycle from max depth. Missing pressure or unconfirmed/incomplete physiology keeps max depth steady, with no NDL label or placeholder. Losing NDL immediately restores max depth at full opacity and cancels the pulse; valid NDL restarts the cycle from max depth. Ceiling and oxygen-exposure details remain in Dive Log. The log area shows ceiling, CNS, rolling 24-hour OTU and ppO2 alongside its NDL summary: current readings for an active profile, recorded maxima for a saved log. These summaries belong to the selected dive, never to a different live session; older logs without recorded exposure show dashes.

Compact and expanded cards share the following rules for a 5 m target. The warning and return depths move with a custom target. The app measures vertical speed over up to five seconds of continuous pressure readings. It needs at least one second of history. Speeds at or above 0.6 m/min in either direction register as motion.

Tap the compact timer or select Safety Stop in the camera menu to expand it. Completion expands the card and displays a green check. OK dismisses the completion. If estimated air reaches reserve before a qualifying stop, the separate gas alert reads **BEGIN PLANNED ASCENT TO SAFETY STOP** and **ESTIMATED AIR LOW. CHECK GAUGE; FOLLOW DIVE PLAN.**

Start and completion use maximum alarm-stream volume, synthesized tones and amplitude-255 vibration waveforms. Start tone duration is 1.8 seconds; completion is 2.4 seconds. Offline English speech says exactly `BEGIN SAFETY STOP` and `SAFETY STOP FINISHED`, at reduced speaking rate. The previous volume is restored after playback if the user has not changed it. Device logs confirmed both spoken phrases finished. Acoustic effectiveness through the housing underwater has not been measured.

The settings column is 126 dp wide, 40% narrower than the previous 210 dp column. Left/right chevrons replace the minus/plus controls. On Dive Logs, LEFT/RIGHT browse and OK opens the selected saved log's menu. Delete Dive Log lives inside that menu; selecting it opens a separate confirmation identifying the saved dive, with Cancel selected by default. LEFT/RIGHT choose Cancel/Delete and OK confirms. Profile points use their recorded elapsed times, including unequal sampling intervals. The chart starts at the first actual reading, retains monitoring gaps, and ends at the final recorded surface depth. ## Normal safety-stop rules

- Start at 1.5 m. End after three minutes of consecutive fresh readings at or shallower than 0.8 m. Brief surfacings remain in one log; exclude the final surface wait from its duration. After at least ten seconds of confirmed surface readings, housing shutdown/sensor loss closes the dive. Loss at depth never assumes surfacing.
- Recommend the optional stop only after exceeding 11 m, independent of the configured hold target, or after a valid NDL-zero obligation. The depth trigger follows the [Tern TX manual, page 25](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0838/3732/1530/files/TernTX_Manual_Rev_B.pdf?v=1709841056). There is no arbitrary minimum dive duration that can override a known decompression obligation. The three-minute surface grouping interval is a logging choice, not a decompression rule.
- Default to 5 m for 180 seconds, configurable to targets 3-6 m and durations 180/300 seconds.
- Arm after the qualifying depth/NDL condition. Start only on an observed ascent reaching or crossing that target; descent through the target never starts the timer. Surface readings alone do not start a timer. An existing shallow warning can remain visible up to confirmed surfacing. Confirmed surfacing archives the outcome.
- Count within the limits and recovery rules above. A boundary comparison allows half one housing pressure step (0.005 kPa) for rounding the sensor packet.
- Credit only consecutive ordered fresh pressure readings, both in the band, less than three seconds apart. No wall-clock tick grants stop time.
- Pause outside the band, on stale/malformed/invalid readings and on disconnect. Preserve earned time without credit across gaps. An unfinished deeper excursion does not restart the timer.
- Completion stays completed without repeated alerts. Rearm only on fresh renewed descent deeper than both target +0.25 m and the shallowest depth since completion +0.25 m. The next observed ascent to target starts a new full stop. This tolerance prevents minor drift from rearming a completed stop.
- Surfacing clears the active stop; the next dive has its own profile and maximum. Restoring a completed checkpoint preserves completion and dismissal. Restoring an unfinished checkpoint resets time credit because the intervening depth was not monitored.

The safety-stop countdown is distinct from a mandatory decompression ceiling. A valid positive ceiling pauses safety-stop credit, forces the existing banner to show a red decompression/ceiling message, and prevents a green completion or OK dismissal from hiding that obligation. Credit resumes only after a fresh interval bracketed by cleared readings; the clearance sample does not credit the previous paused interval. If monitoring is interrupted after a known ceiling, the hold remains and the card directs the diver to the backup computer. Restarting reconstructs this hold from the retained tissue checkpoint. This is not a staged decompression schedule or a time-to-surface planner. Completion never erases tissue loading or oxygen exposure.

PADI describes a ceiling as a depth the diver must stay below and explains that computers use their own decompression algorithms. The app uses the Bühlmann configuration documented below. Its custom stop bands are product behaviour. PADI certification of this implementation has not been established. [PADI, How Dive Computers Work](https://www.padi.com/us/en/blog/how-dive-computers-work)

## NDL, ceilings and oxygen

`Buhlmann.kt` implements open-circuit ZH-L16C, all 16 nitrogen and helium compartments, pressure-weighted mixed-gas a/b coefficients, and exact Schreiner integration for linear pressure changes. `expm1` stabilizes short sensor intervals. Default gradient factors are 40/85. NDL projects a 9 m/min ascent on the selected gas and checks both the ascent path and surface arrival against GF high. It reports additional whole seconds at the current depth, capped at 99 minutes. The displayed minute value rounds down; the displayed ceiling rounds up to a tenth of the selected depth unit.

The decompression ceiling uses a retained first-stop anchor rounded up to a 3 m multiple of the GF-low ceiling, with GF increasing linearly to GF high at the surface. This is a ceiling calculation, not a complete staged decompression schedule or time-to-surface planner. There are no CCR/setpoint, altitude, salinity or in-dive gas-switch controls. Configuration currently follows the established depth conversion: surface 1.01325 bar, freshwater 0.0981 bar/m, and water-vapour pressure 0.0627 bar. The settings display identifies the model, gradient factors and sea-level/freshwater assumption.

Oxygen exposure includes current ppO2 in ATA, NOAA CNS percentage, and rolling 24-hour OTU. CNS rate is interpolated between NOAA table knots and integrated exactly across pressure ramps; pulmonary OTU uses the 5/6-power formula. Above 1.6 ATA the CNS table is not extrapolated: the accumulated table-rate value is explicitly displayed as a lower bound, retaining an out-of-range exposure flag. CNS recovery uses a 90-minute half-time during confirmed surface air intervals. A selected oxygen-rich gas is not silently changed to air at shallow depth. OTU uses minute bins and retains the entire boundary minute, conservatively over-retaining by less than a minute.

Tissue and oxygen state persist independently of the 1,000-log retention limit. First use and legacy checkpoints have unknown history and show dashes for NDL/ceiling/CNS/OTU. A one-time pre-dive confirmation lets the diver explicitly declare no diving or supplemental oxygen exposure in the preceding 48 hours, with a fresh surface reading, before initializing equilibrium tissues. An unmonitored interval after at least 60 seconds at the surface requires confirmation that the entire interval was spent at the surface breathing air before off-gassing is credited. An underwater monitoring gap, clock inconsistency or unlogged immersion suppresses numerical limits; it never silently resets tissues to a clean dive. Current ppO2 can still be computed from a fresh pressure reading and the declared gas.

Surface intervals use the actual elapsed time. Continuous readings integrate every nitrogen and helium compartment at the observed pressure on the declared gas. Across a restart or housing disconnection, the saved integration timestamp identifies the uncredited interval; after the surface-air confirmation, the model integrates that entire interval on air and advances the timestamp exactly once. The next dive starts from those residual compartment pressures, not from equilibrium or the previous dive's unchanged final loading. CNS surface recovery and rolling OTU expiry also use elapsed time. Regression tests replay the independent repetitive-air fixture both continuously and across a restart, compare 30/60/120-minute intervals and subsequent NDL, and verify that repeated confirmations/restarts cannot double-credit nitrogen, helium or CNS recovery.


## Persistence and housing shutdown

After the housing power-off instruction clears, the app displays a dim black surface and keeps its foreground window awake. A new ready housing connection restores normal brightness and the dive controls.

AtomicFile stores a bounded version-6 checkpoint in the existing `dive-profile-v1.bin` location. Versions 1–5 remain readable. Version 6 adds the continuation state and diver confirmation record. Versions before 4 have no recorded minimum NDL, and versions before 5 have no recorded ceiling/oxygen summary. Version-1 settings and logs migrate with uninitialized physiology; no tissue loading is fabricated. The byte limit is 64 MB, sufficient for 1,000 full 3,600-point logs, one active profile, and tissue/oxygen history. At capacity, archiving replaces only the oldest saved log. A full-capacity codec test verifies every log's point count, endpoints and NDL after reloading. Writes are conflated at five-second intervals plus teardown, so abrupt process death can lose the latest five seconds. Old monotonic timestamps are never restored. Each profile keeps up to 3,600 plotted samples, reducing long profiles while preserving gaps. Monitoring runs while the app's ViewModel lives.

After a verified post-dive surface vent, a centered banner offers SHUT DOWN on the left and RESTART PUMP on the right. SHUT DOWN is selected by default, including after process restart and each new vent. Left/Right select a choice; touching either tile only selects it. With SHUT DOWN selected, short and repeated OK events are consumed without starting the pump; the housing firmware handles holding OK to power off. With RESTART PUMP selected, a short OK explicitly starts the normal seal-check workflow with cover-open gating, motor timeout and automatic stop conditions. Restart is blocked when disconnected, when the cover is closed/unknown or when the available water-pressure reading indicates submersion. Direct pump-start commands and seal-reset bypasses remain blocked while the choice is pending. Reconnection resets the selection and awaits fresh cover telemetry.

After reconnecting with the vacuum released and the cap open, the normal pump-start banner reads `INSERT PHONE INTO HOUSING THEN`, `PRESS MENU/OK TO START VACUUM PUMP`, and `DOWN dismisses`. OK enters the managed seal-check workflow; DOWN dismisses the prompt. A still-established vacuum is adopted instead of offering a pump start.

Reference library code is not bundled in the app. Ten profiles cover Air, Nitrox, Trimix, staged gas switches in the numerical engine, repetitive dives and very short pressure ramps. All 32 tissue pressures and GF40/GF85 ceilings agree within 1e-9 bar; whole-second NDL matches the reference cases. Additional tests cover oxygen dose integration, monitoring gaps, helium, offline intervals, clock changes, persistence and legacy migration.

Safety-stop tests cover threshold boundaries, descent rejection, ascent triggering, valid time credit, drift, missing/out-of-order packets, restart, actual surface trigger, minimization, menu restoration and completion dismissal. Phone verification covered the full and minimized UI, active-only menu restoration, speech completion, same-size heading and filled warning triangles. Real vent/power-cycle behavior and underwater audio remain unverified on hardware.


## October 1 field regressions

The phone contained four records for the user's two shallow dives: max depths 2.34, 2.52, 4.82 and 1.64 m, with a 4 m stop target. The afternoon fragments had surface gaps of 155.967 and 29.522 seconds. Crossing the configured target incorrectly qualified the 4.82 m segment; immediate archival on a zero reading split the session. The new surface interval and independent qualification address both cases.

Air and stop panels share one measured stack. A reminder or pressure-entry card compacts the stop directly above it, retaining any decompression ceiling. Its OK belongs to the air card; an expanded optional stop has its own OK MINIMIZE button. Shutter in the camera always retains its capture action. Active recording removes alert backgrounds/scrims while retaining text and controls; pausing restores the configured opacity. Timers and alarms continue.

At a surface disconnect, an established seal becomes a candidate for fresh revalidation instead of carrying its leak-sample window into the next connection. Two agreeing ambient readings plus current external surface pressure discard the previous vacuum verdict and require a new check, without a false seal-failure alert. Matching vacuum restores earned confidence; weakened vacuum and underwater loss remain failure paths. Seal telemetry cannot replay the intro over a running session.
