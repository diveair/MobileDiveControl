# Nauticase performance and heat audit

Galaxy S24 SM-S921W · 23 November 2026 · Installed debuggable build · Housing disconnected

The subsequent audit ranks all 21 camera mode IDs, 194 mode-specific controls and 5,913 source entries. This first audit contains the controlled compass and pointing comparisons. [Complete optimization ranking](performance/OPTIMIZATION_RANKING.md)

## What happens when the phone sits untouched

On rear 1× Portrait with Samsung Blur 4 and Full aspect, Nauticase consumed **76% of one CPU core** and Samsung’s camera provider **107%**. On the empty Gallery album grid, the camera closed and Nauticase still consumed **20.5%**. The Gallery result gives the project a place to reduce heat even when there is no viewfinder, no media playing and no housing sending pressure samples.

| Stationary screen | Nauticase CPU | Samsung camera provider | Android camera server | SurfaceFlinger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Screen dozing, launcher behind lock screen | 0.0% in three short readings | No active camera client | — | — |
| Portrait, disconnected banner visible | 76.25% | 107% | 9% | 11.5% |
| Gallery, zero albums, no playback | 20.5% | No active camera client, one 0.5% reading | Below displayed process list | 2.75% |

Portrait and Gallery figures are medians of eight subsequent `top` readings approximately two seconds apart. The initial reading was discarded. A process can use several cores. SurfaceFlinger is the phone’s shared compositor. These figures omit GPU, image signal processor, display panel and radio energy. The S24 reported external power, brightness 0.404 in both foreground runs, a battery temperature of 40.7 °C near the Portrait sample, and a skin reading that later reached 42 °C. Android thermal status moved from light to moderate. Charging, prior use and the delay between chip and case temperature prevent assigning that temperature rise to any single feature.

## Which features caused the idle CPU load?

I used a temporary Android instrumentation package to exercise the installed app. The first comparison switched the normal ViewModel compass subscription off and back on. The pointing comparison used a production `CameraRuntimeController`, with the HUD removed for all three readings. It switched only the pointing callback on the same running Samsung session, checked that the preview kept producing frames, and left compass monitoring paused throughout. Each reading had a 3.2-second settling period and an 8.5-second measurement window. Values are total app process CPU time divided by elapsed time, with 100% representing one busy CPU core.

| Screen and one changed input | Enabled | Temporarily paused | Enabled again | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Gallery, compass updates | 32.0% | **4.3%** | 28.5% | 24–28 percentage points of app CPU followed the compass and the UI work its updates trigger. |
| Diagnostics, compass updates | 24.3% | **1.2%** | — | A 23.1-point difference on a screen that hides the camera HUD. One on/off pair was collected. |
| Portrait with full UI, compass updates | 59.8% | **43.4%** | 59.8% | A repeatable 16.4-point difference while the live Samsung blur continued. |
| Portrait preview only, pointing inference | 38.3% | **32.3%** | 35.5% | A 3–6-point app CPU difference. The separate GPU cost of readback and inference is unknown. |
| Ordinary Photo with full UI and compass | 77.1% | — | — | A separate sample with the user's Photo settings. Ordinary preview also sustained heavy app activity. |

The controlled readings and the earlier untouched-screen readings came from separate runs with different warmup histories. Compare enabled and paused conditions **within a row**. Pausing the compass removes both sensor computation and downstream UI invalidation. The stack profiles identify where that time went. The measurements do not predict how much a narrower subscription alone will save.

## Highest-impact paths for a screen left open

The order reflects the observed workload, how long each path runs, and how much of it the app can avoid. Rows overlap. Camera binding starts work in several other processes, and an inclusive CPU sample also contains its callees. Adding the rows would double count work.

| Priority | Path and when it runs | Evidence and first optimization to evaluate |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Camera, Samsung bokeh and display surfaces.** `CameraRuntimeController.bindCamera`, `SamsungPortraitBokeh.resolve`, the frame-update callback and CameraX. | This was the largest measured system CPU load. It runs whenever Portrait is visibly live, including behind the disconnected banner. `DiveControlScreen` also composes live content underneath the opaque welcome, intro and cap-prompt layers when the underlying destination is Camera. Defer the graph while a genuinely opaque, potentially indefinite prompt completely hides it. Prewarm near the handoff and measure first-frame and first-shutter latency. A banner over a usable viewfinder calls for a different policy. |
| 2 | **Repeated HUD and full-screen UI updates.** `MainActivity.onCreate` collects the entire `CompassReading` in the root composition. `DiveControlScreen` forwards it through `DiveControlContent` and `CameraHudOverlay`. `StateDrivenCameraPreview` also keys `applyState` on heading and safety state. | In Gallery, `checkForSemanticsChanges` appeared on **38.76%** of sampled app userspace CPU stacks, `Recomposer.performRecompose` on **27.04%**, `CameraHudOverlay` on **14.33%**, and `GalleryBrowser` on **10.10%**. In Portrait, `CameraHudOverlay` accounted for **6.56%** inclusive and the bottom tray **4.17%**. Collect precise heading where the compass or arrow draws. Give the numeric label its rounded value and make the camera controller consume only the inputs a control change needs. Keep the accessibility tree and housing permission controls fully functional. |
| 3 | **Pointing recognition on an unattended preview.** `sampleVendorPortraitGesture`, `PointingGestureRecognizer.analyzePreviewBitmap`, `recognizeForVideo`. On ordinary analysis-capable cameras the entry point is `PointingGestureRecognizer.analyze`. | The S24’s Samsung extension supplies no analysis stream. Nauticase calls `TextureView.getBitmap` on the main thread, allocates a bitmap up to 320 px wide or tall, and submits it to MediaPipe up to 10 times per second. The normal stream gates inference at 80 ms. The phone logged **21 ms average and 38 ms maximum** for 30 post-warmup recognitions. This is elapsed inference time. The MediaPipe GL worker alone accounted for **12.07%** of one app userspace CPU sample. Reuse buffers with explicit ownership, avoid inference when input cannot be acted on, then test a cheaper search rate that accelerates upon detecting a candidate hand. Preserve the current confirmation cadence once tracking starts. |
| 4 | **Display kept awake across foreground destinations.** `MainActivity.onCreate` applies `FLAG_KEEP_SCREEN_ON` to the Activity. | Common to Gallery, settings and the camera. There is no isolated display-power reading in this audit. Consider an opt-in surface-idle dimming policy for static screens only after measuring power at fixed luminance. Preserve underwater legibility, visible alarms and immediate controls. Removing redundant work in rows 1–3 requires no brightness change. |
| 5 | **Shared analysis stream and preview compositor in ordinary camera modes.** `bindCamera` requests 640 × 480 RGBA in the typical graph, `setAnalyzer` dispatches its consumers, and `FocusPeakingSurfaceProcessor.draw` swaps an output per frame in compatible JPEG-preview graphs. | Turning off the peaking indicator does not by itself remove this pass. CameraX can still be delivering RGBA on frames that no pixel consumer needs. Evaluate a direct preview path and a cheaper analysis format in simple modes. The active analysis stream also carries focus/lens telemetry, and the GL path owns frame continuity. Preserve both before removing a stream or effect. This is a separate path from the S24’s native Portrait blur. |
| 6 | **Sensor processing, per-frame metadata and camera controls.** `CompassHeadingMonitor.onSensorChanged` processes gravity, magnetic field and gyro. `CameraRuntimeController.attach` registers a separate focus-motion listener. Ordinary `onCaptureCompleted` handles AF and throttles the slower telemetry block. | The compass requests game-rate gravity and gyro, magnetic samples every 20 ms, and publishes the heading and camera basis at most every 100 ms. `onSensorChanged` alone appeared on **4.89%** of Gallery and **2.09%** of Portrait app userspace stacks. The Activity stops compass monitoring in `onStop`. Scope subscriptions to visible consumers, retain full-rate stabilization needed for navigation, and avoid resubmitting camera options when only a display value changed. |
| 7 | **Visible animation, composition and media playback.** `IntroCarouselScreen`, `SealCapPromptScreen`, `HousingDiagram`, conditional Safety pulses, HUD clock and `LoopingVideoTextureView`. | The running carousel and cap-prompt animation can tick with vsync. The initial held plate and the carousel have different conditions. Clocks and ordinary countdown text update much less often. A playing video review decodes continuously. Restrict animation invalidation to the drawing layer and release playback when obscured. Preserve the actual banner deadlines and the timing of button acknowledgements. |
| 8 | **Housing discovery, reconnection and air receiver.** `HousingLink.supervise`, `AndroidBleTransport.scan`, `AirPressureReceiver`, GATT callbacks and the foreground link service. | Disconnected discovery can wake the radio while a screen is idle. First discovery uses low-latency scanning and a remembered address uses low-power scanning. The housing scan window is 12 seconds and retries back off. A separate air-receiver scan is time-limited. Audit scan duty cycle over several reconnect periods. The disconnected measurements do not establish radio energy or real connected traffic. Preserve shutdown recognition and reconnect latency. |
| 9 | **Gallery loading, thumbnail queries, preferences and diagnostic logs.** `GalleryRepository.loadItems` and `loadRecordingSaveLocations` enumerate images and videos, group albums or filter the requested folder. `LatestCaptureProvider` observes changes. `DiveProfileStore.save` writes a snapshot. | Gallery entry can become expensive with a large user library. The stationary test contained zero albums. Its UI cost should not be attributed to indexing photos. Push predicates and projections into MediaStore and invalidate affected folders on changes. The latest-capture query already limits results. Camera-setting writes are conflated and rate limited, profile saves are spaced, and raw pressure logging is gated on Diagnostics. |
| 10 | **Regular dive, housing and input work.** `collectDiveProfile`, `ControlCore.tickDiveProfile`, `HousingSensorMonitor.run`, the protocol parser, button router, seal transitions, `SafetyStateMachine.apply`, alert scheduling and notification handling. | The ViewModel wakes every 500 ms for the dive tick. `DiveProfile.tick` returns immediately when its sensor is unavailable. Connected water/barometric fallback reads are scheduled at 500/1,000 ms in the normal policy, adjusted for recent valid notifications and the pressure-only state. The pump watchdog is a delayed timeout. These mechanisms did not explain the disconnected heat. Preserve freshness checks, pump responses, both vacuum timers, verdicts and alert deadlines. |

### How the ranking changes with the screen

| Destination | Work to expect when left open |
|---|---|
| Photo, Pro, Food, Night, ordinary Expert RAW | A live preview, with the selected camera graph, shared analysis and permitted display effects. Ordinary Photo was measured. Expert stacking, still encoding and Astro processing need a capture. Sky Guide adds sensors and location when selected. |
| Portrait | Supported Blur routes through Samsung for preview and stills. Other supported effects or fallback lenses can invoke Nauticase’s segmentation and GPU pipeline. The live native Blur and its pointing readback were measured. |
| Panorama | The code requests **4000 × 3000 YUV analysis** when entering Panorama, even before a sweep, and decodes an idle thumbnail at up to 10 Hz. Hardware negotiates the actual size. This is a high-priority standby measurement. Evaluate a smaller idle stream only if the first sweep frame, response to the shutter and image quality survive the transition. |
| Video, Pro Video, Portrait Video, Slow Motion, Hyperlapse | The selected path determines which surfaces are armed. A prepared high-speed graph, software Portrait Video segmentation and a running night accumulator have different standby costs. A direct Camera2 selection also has a separate 30 fps idle CameraX policy. Inspect the current graph before assuming that selecting 8K or 240 fps captures at that rate while waiting. |
| Camera options, recording destination chooser, onboarding | Camera adjustments share one camera shell. Selecting settings can leave the viewfinder intentionally available. Full-screen opaque layers can conceal work for much longer than their transition. Activity backgrounding, an opaque in-app page and a banner over the camera are three different cases. |
| Safety, Gallery, Diagnostics, Dive Settings, phone cursor and target | These route to distinct non-camera destinations. Gallery confirmed the camera closed. Diagnostics and Dive Settings hide the camera HUD. A dive-alert simulation deliberately previews alerts over a real camera. Safety animations, gallery playback and diagnostic packet collection are conditional on what that screen is doing. |

## Workloads that can dominate during capture

This second order is an engineering estimate of **peak or sustained cost while the feature is active**. It uses the processing performed, pixel count and duration. No high-resolution recording or simulated dive was run to obtain these estimates. The relative order of the first three depends on resolution, frame rate, hardware acceleration and recording duration.

| Estimated order | Functions and workload | Opportunity with the existing feature preserved |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | `startRecordingSegmentNow`, `bindPreparedRecorder`, `PreparedCamera2Recorder.prepare/start`, `Camera2EightKRecorder.start`. 8K, high-frame-rate slow motion, 10-bit HDR/Log and high-resolution stabilized video drive the sensor, ISP, codecs, memory and storage. Prepared high-speed or native Log configurations can also target the recorder surface during standby. | Compare **armed preview** and actual recording independently. Keep full file resolution, cadence, dynamic range, native Log metadata and instant shutter behavior. Look first for recorder surfaces required only while recording. |
| 2 | Software Portrait Video, optional software Portrait still looks and night hyperlapse. `PortraitSegmenter.analyze` samples 256 × 144 RGBA, converts a confidence mask and passes it to the shader for upload. Some Portrait Video effects perform 16 texture taps per output pixel. `PortraitLiveTracker.submit` produces software still masks, `PortraitBackgroundBlur.render` builds the blurred layer, and `HyperlapseNightAccumulator.accumulate/renderPreview/renderEncoder` performs multiple render passes. | Reuse mask buffers after upload, share intermediate work between preview and encoder where geometry permits, and discard superseded work before conversion. Keep the existing Samsung path on supported still lenses. Validate silhouettes around a moving person and preview/capture agreement. |
| 3 | `captureExpertRawPhoto`, `processExpertFrames`, `combineJpegs`, `blendInto`, `applyVirtualAperture`, and still-image encoding. ND can involve up to 24 frames and Astro up to 30 spread over the chosen duration. `PhotoFileEncoder.publish` handles additional formats through full bitmap decode and encoding. | Profile CPU ms, peak native memory and bytes reread per image. Use tiles or reusable row buffers where they remove full-image passes. Compare final pixels, metadata, crop and interrupted-capture cleanup. |
| 4 | `SamsungPanoramaEngine.process`, `decodePanoramaSampledFrame`, `updatePanoramaContinuousThumbnail`, `queuePanoramaComposition`, `PanoramaBitmapStitcher.Incremental.append/finish`. Native and fallback routes have different costs. The fallback also builds an idle preview strip at up to 10 Hz. | Measure sweep and finalization separately, reuse thumbnail buffers and avoid decoding the same keyframe twice. Check seam quality, direction, live guidance and full-resolution output. |
| 5 | `HighSpeedVideoTranscoder.transcode`, `buildCumulativeRecordingReview`, `RecordingSessionMuxer.buildReview`, `retimeHighSpeed` and `copySegment`. Fractional frame selection may decode and re-encode. Other paths remux timestamps. Rebuilding a cumulative multi-segment review can repeatedly copy earlier clips after successive pauses. | Reuse finalized segments or play the segment sequence directly for review, flattening once when publishing. Preserve audio sync, selected playback cadence, pause/resume controls, and the existing single-segment fast path. For a long clip, this can outlast the user’s time on the recording screen. |
| 6 | Underwater white balance, AF, cinematic exposure, Sky Guide, metering and display assists. `UnderwaterFrameAnalyzer.analyze` samples on an 8-pixel grid at up to 8 Hz, and the normal underwater request interval is 250 ms. Sky Guide can register motion and request a location fix. | Profile each feature enabled and disabled on the same camera graph. Reuse histograms and skip redundant requests while preserving colour transitions, focus acquisition/hold, zebra thresholds and Sky Guide response. |
| 7 | Connected-dive calculations, gas alarms, dive-log persistence and playback. `Decompression.sample/calculate`, `Buhlmann.ndlSeconds`, `DiveProfile.sample`, `DiveProfileChart` and `DiveStopNotifier`. Tissues are modelled in 16 compartments and NDL searches forward. Alarm sound, speech and vibration run when triggered. | Replay recorded synthetic pressure traces and compare every numerical output, freshness decision and alarm timestamp. Profile long-history persistence and chart drawing separately. Leave numerical and timing behavior intact. |
| 8 | Single ordinary photo, file operations, permissions, onboarding transitions, mode selection, button decoding, battery updates and preference changes. | Most are event-driven. Optimize only when a trace shows a repeated trigger or a long burst. `onAccessibilityEvent` already filters system packages. The high Gallery accessibility cost appeared in Compose’s semantics update path inside the app. |

## Implementation order and verification

1. **Reduce the scope of compass updates first.** Collect display heading and the full camera basis at their respective consumers. The arrow can keep full precision and the same refresh rate. Start or stop sensors according to active consumers, including any camera feature using heading. For the first comparison, aim to bring stationary Diagnostics and empty Gallery toward the measured **1–5%** range on this debug setup. Recheck arrow smoothness, numerical heading, pointing, target persistence and returning to Camera. The paused-compass comparison sets an upper bound on what this change could recover in that session. [Activity collection](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/MainActivity.kt#L181) · [HUD consumers](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/CameraHudOverlay.kt#L178) · [Camera state effect](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/camera/StateDrivenCameraPreview.kt#L161) · [Compose performance guidance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/performance/bestpractices)
2. **Stop doing camera work behind long-lived, fully opaque prompts.** Validate with the camera service’s active-client list and app/provider CPU on the actual prompt. Test a clean launch, repeat launch, early button dismissal, connecting/connected transitions and time to the first usable shutter. Keep live and partially visible views interactive. [Screen routing and layers](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/DiveControlScreen.kt#L94)
3. **Reduce the cost of finding a pointing hand.** Evaluate bitmap reuse first and adaptive search second. Measure whole-device energy, app CPU, frame pacing, time to first accepted point, false positives and reacquisition under dim light. The current recognizer uses timing and multi-frame confirmation, so simply reducing the inference rate can make the controls feel slower. [Samsung preview sampling](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/camera/CameraRuntimeController.kt#L9978) · [Recognizer](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/camera/PointingGestureRecognizer.kt#L64)
4. **Profile each standby camera graph before altering it.** Prioritize Panorama, ordinary Photo and prepared video modes. Test graph changes independently of focus, colour and still/video quality. Record time to first frame and first shutter, preview frame-interval percentiles, dropped recording frames, actual saved format, sound sync and every supported lens. Preserve the original camera configuration across relaunch. [Shared analyzer](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/camera/CameraRuntimeController.kt#L4074) · [GPU output](../app/src/main/kotlin/com/mobiledivecontrol/ui/camera/FocusPeakingSurfaceProcessor.kt#L436)
5. **Verify housing and thermal behavior separately.** Repeat with the same external-power condition, then compare on battery from a common starting temperature for 10–15 minutes per screen. Alternate run order. Include connected housing, both vacuum countdowns and PASSED/FAILED presentation, leak/interruption paths, button response and reconnection. These are regression checks for later implementation. This audit sampled a disconnected housing and made no production change.

## Coverage and interpretation

The source inventory spans **157 Kotlin and two Java files** under `app/src/main` and `core/src/main`, including main-source debug and simulator code. The companion CSV lists **2,028 named declarations** with file, line and feature family. The Kotlin compiler’s syntax tree supplies the inventory. The two Java methods are vendor-interface callbacks. Anonymous lambdas, implicit constructors, generated getters, native shaders, framework internals and Samsung services have no independent source-declaration row. The tables above describe those paths where relevant. [Function index](PERFORMANCE_FUNCTION_INDEX.csv)

CPU profiling used Android `simpleperf` on Nauticase. A 12-second flat Portrait capture recorded 580 userspace samples. The call-stack captures recorded 671 Portrait samples in 14 seconds and 307 Gallery samples in 14 seconds. Each source percentage is the proportion of that screen’s sampled **app userspace CPU**, including child calls unless marked “self”. Captures ran separately from the `top` measurements. The CSV exposes exact unambiguous symbol matches above the report’s 0.35% threshold, with separate rankings per screen. Its first rows are ordered by approximate observed userspace CPU per elapsed second across the two runs. A blank can indicate inlining, an unmatched callback, an inactive path or too few samples. Twenty-three named source declarations resolved directly. The remainder are indexed for follow-up. A binding function can therefore have a blank CPU cell and still be responsible for the highest ongoing hardware cost.

The evidence archive contains the process samples, symbol profiles, controlled comparison output and the temporary runner source. The runner returned success, its test package was removed, and the saved camera preference fields matched the pre-test snapshot. The installed app reopened in Portrait at 1× and Blur 4. [Measurement evidence](S24_PERFORMANCE_EVIDENCE.zip)

Hardware power attribution needs an external meter or supported per-rail energy counters. CPU sampling alone cannot tell how many watts a function uses or predict a case-temperature reduction. Repeat release-like builds at matched brightness, same scene, same housing and charging state, and comparable starting temperature. Record energy, thermal status, app and camera-service CPU, preview frame intervals and dropped frames together. Use Android’s thermal status as a device-wide signal. [Android thermal API](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal)
